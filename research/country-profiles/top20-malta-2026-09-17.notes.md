It explicitly says the former investment framework was repealed following the 29 April 2025 CJEU judgment and rejects presenting merit as its continuation or alternative. The candidate is an informational ordinary-route baseline, not marketing for merit.
- [MTCA Tax Residence](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/individual/tax-residence): actual HTML read, marked updated 29 August 2025. It states more than 183 days, not 183 or more; settlement on arrival is a separate route. Ordinary residence can persist through temporary absence and can arise through lasting ties despite shorter annual stays. Registration and tax returns remain separate obligations.
- [Income Tax Act, Cap. 123](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/69d8a9187da37f0580d5140c): actual 218-page English consolidation opened and relevant provisions read. Reached through MTCA's Act link, [official current landing page](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng), then its PDF viewer. Includes Act III of 2026; landing page shows publication 10 March 2026. Article 4(1), printed pages 14–16/PDF indices 13–15, establishes the worldwide starting scope, foreign-income/remittance and foreign-capital-gain provisos, and the precisely defined permanent/long-term status and spouse exceptions. Article 56(27), printed page 187/PDF index 186, confines the EUR 5,000 floor to an ordinarily resident non-domiciled remittance-basis individual meeting its income conditions. It includes spouses assessed under Article 49, excludes another statutory scheme already imposing a minimum, credits Maltese tax other than Article 5A property-transfer tax, and permits proof of a lower worldwide liability. No 2027 rate change elsewhere in the consolidation was represented as currently applicable.
- [MTCA remittance guidance](https://mtca.gov.mt/docs/default-source/documents/mtca-guidelines-on-the-remittance-under-the-income-tax.pdf): actual seven-page PDF read, with body text on printed pages 2–6. Sections 1.2–1.7 explain worldwide/remittance/source scope and spouse/civil-union qualifications; sections 2–3 distinguish residence and domicile; sections 4–5 discuss source, foreign capital and proof of remittances; sections 6.2–6.6 explain minimum tax and relief. No internal publication date is asserted. The current Act corroborates the substantive statutory claims instead of treating an undated guidance note as a new 2026 law.

## Review-sensitive choices

- Citizenship age uses the Act's attained-18 definition rather than converting the Agency's informal over-18 wording into an age-19 rule.
- The structured four-year constraint describes only the prior six-year segment. Its cohort and prose retain the additional final twelve months and exceptional earlier-residence discretion. It is not a freestanding four-year citizenship route.
- Adequate Maltese or English is not converted into a CEFR score. All ordinary-route cases remain subject to ministerial suitability assessment.
- No generic absence allowance or automatic processing deadline is claimed. No application-fee or exceptional-merit fast-track comparison is included.
- Permanent-residence exceptions are restricted to the two named subsidiary-law statuses; no broad claim that every advertised permanent-residence product cancels non-dom taxation.
- The statutory minimum-tax cohort is narrower than the guidance's abbreviated non-domiciled headline: ordinary residence, the remittance provisos, foreign income not fully remitted and no separate minimum-tax scheme must all be checked.
- Foreign capital gains receive the stated treatment only in the remittance context. Foreign-paid earnings for work performed in Malta are not automatically foreign-source.
- Source text was paraphrased; the candidate contains no verbatim source excerpts. No private adviser, commercial programme page or search snippet supplies a published claim.
