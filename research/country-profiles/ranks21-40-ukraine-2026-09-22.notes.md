## Important qualifications

The ordinary five-year clock uses the Act's permanent/continuous-residence definition; it is not five years holding any temporary status. Ninety days per private trip and 180 total in a year coexist with documented-purpose exceptions. The two-year examination undertaking is a deferral for specific cohorts, not a waiver.

The new multiple-nationality framework is not universal. Applicable Article 9 requirements remain, mixed-nationality cases need separate undertakings, and Article 19 loss grounds still exist. A domestic declaration does not establish the effect under foreign law. The Tax Code's surviving credit restriction is expressly for other nationality held **contrary to law**; it cannot be broadened to every lawful dual national after the citizenship reform.

Tax residence is a hierarchy with home/centre-of-life grounds before day count and citizenship fallback, plus the provision's other sufficient grounds. Electronic-resident status alone is not enough. Foreign credits have evidence, treaty, cap and excluded-tax limitations.
