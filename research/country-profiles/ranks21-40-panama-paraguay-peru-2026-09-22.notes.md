## Material qualifications and unresolved points

- Panama's constitutional five consecutive years and required permanent-resident documentation do not, in the collected operative material, settle the exact start of the qualifying residence clock or a full absence-counting rule. The candidate deliberately has no unqualified structured numeric residence constraint for Panama.
- Panama's fiscal sourcing summary is not a full consolidated 2026 Fiscal Code. The actual 2014 restoration, later 2015 amendment and currently posted individual-return guidance are read together. The foreign-service example is non-exhaustive; the candidate expressly preserves the other payer rules in its limitations. Corporate multinational/substance developments are excluded rather than projected onto individuals.
- A proposed Fiscal Code copy at `www.denunciaciudadana.gob.pa/dc/assets/codigo-fiscal2.pdf` failed TLS hostname verification. It was abandoned, not cited or treated as current law, and TLS verification was not disabled.
- Paraguay's currently posted tax-residence FAQ still names Migration Law 978/1996 and amendments, although later migration legislation exists. The fact is attributed to DNIT's published tax criterion and its Decree 3181 reference; it does not declare the old migration law wholly current or assert automatic foreign treaty residence. No unsupported universal 120-day/183-day rule is introduced.
- Paraguay's Acordada absence condition is kept in the actual “periods exceeding three months per year during each of the three preceding years” wording. No conversion to 90 days or resolution of unusual absences is invented. Current court guidance remains the procedural anchor; this bounded review is not an exhaustive consolidation of every court-rule amendment.
- Peru's Law 32421 is enacted but its third final provision makes commencement depend on publication of its regulation. The reviewed January 2026 application guidance still says two years and the actual congressional tracker has a deadline without a regulation entry. Neither source establishes that the trigger had occurred by 22 September 2026. The candidate marks commencement as `not_established`, attributes the two-year description to the posted procedure, and does not encode the new five years as an operative numeric constraint.
- An older Peru naturalisation requirements PDF with 2015 material and an aggregate 183-day absence statement was inspected during discovery but rejected as a current-source anchor. The current page instead refers to the immigration regulation's relevant loss/absence permissions. No obsolete absence rule is carried forward.
- No CEFR level, general test exemption, foreign-law effect of renunciation, guarantee of approval, flat tax-rate comparison or remittance exemption is inferred. Peru's new-law language wording is not presented as current in advance of resolving commencement.
- Web screenshot calls did not produce inspectable images in the tool output used here. Scanned legal pages described above were actually downloaded and read through Spanish OCR; this is not claimed as visual manual verification of screenshots. No downloaded document was added to the repository.
