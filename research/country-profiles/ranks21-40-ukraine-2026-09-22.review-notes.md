## Substantive checks

The ordinary five-year condition is continuous residence as currently defined through permanent residence, not merely any five years of temporary presence. The separate ninety-day per-private-trip and 180-day annual limits coexist with documented business, study, treatment and rehabilitation exceptions. The structured constraint's `permanent_residence` basis is supported. No guarantee of immigration-permit availability is made.

Language/civics exemptions and deferred examinations are separate. The two-year commitment applies to specified cohorts and is not a general waiver. Nationality recognition, renunciation/termination documents and mixed-nationality cases remain conditional. Article 19 retains loss grounds with exceptions; the text correctly retains the presidential-decree timing rather than claiming automatic loss on every foreign acquisition.

The tax paragraph preserves the sequence of residence, permanent home, centre of vital interests, day count and citizenship fallback, plus the Code's other sufficient grounds. Both arrival and departure days count for the 183-day fallback; e-resident status alone does not establish the centre of vital interests. Passport ownership is not recast as universal citizenship-based taxation.

Resident foreign income and nonresident Ukrainian-source income are correctly distinguished. Foreign-tax relief is subject to treaty provisions, official evidence/legalisation, caps and excluded charges. The departure filing obligation is no later than sixty calendar days before departure, not a date at which tax residence automatically ends.

The nationality-credit restriction in Article 14.1.213 expressly concerns holding another nationality contrary to law. The candidate appropriately notes the newly recognised lawful multiple-nationality cases without guaranteeing an individual's credit or treaty outcome. Its limitation is a necessary scope distinction, not a declaration that the remaining tax clause is repealed.
